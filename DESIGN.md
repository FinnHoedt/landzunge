Finn's Landzunge: Design Documentation
This document outlines the visual system and user experience for the experimental digital art exhibition and portfolio, Finn's Landzunge.

1. Vision & Core Concept
   Finn's Landzunge is a digital exploration of a rogue geographical peninsula in a post-industrial lake. The site functions as a high-end digital museum installation, blending Avant-Garde Brutalism with high-fashion editorial design. It rejects traditional web patterns in favor of a stark, mysterious, and data-driven environment.

2. Visual System (Design Tokens)
   2.1 Colors
   Background: #000000 (Pure Black)
   Primary Text: #FFFFFF (Stark White)
   Accent/Action: #CCFF00 (Neon Acid Green) — Used strictly for hover states, active cursors, and the primary call-to-action.
   2.2 Typography
   Headlines: Massive, heavy, tightly-spaced sans-serif (Inter Black or Helvetica Neue Heavy). Headers should feel architectural and imposing.
   Body & Data: Tiny, strictly gridded monospace (Space Mono or Courier). Used for metadata, exhibition plaques, and visitor entries to mimic technical blueprints.
   2.3 Imagery
   Treatment: Strictly high-contrast, grainy black-and-white.
   Subject Matter: Water ripples, raw dirt, roots, and brutalist concrete structures.
   Integration: Images are layered with data and text, often overlapping or being used as full-bleed textures to create a "fused" digital-nature experience.
3. Key Screen Architectures
   3.1 The Entrance (Hero)
   The entry point is designed to be cryptic. A full-bleed grainy video or image of rippling water serves as the backdrop. The only navigation is the central GPS coordinates in massive typography. Entry is granted via a tiny, blinking neon button at the bottom center.

3.2 Live Weather Monument (Data Art)
This screen treats environmental data as a monumental art piece. Live wind speed and temperature are displayed in screen-filling typography. To integrate the nature aspect, these numbers are layered over high-contrast B&W imagery using transparency and grain, making the data feel part of the landscape.

3.3 Exhibition Plaque (About)
An asymmetrical layout that mimics a museum's informational wall. It uses heavy negative space and thin white grid lines. It incorporates narrow vertical "slivers" of nature photography alongside technical descriptions of the land’s history.

3.4 Multimedia Visitor Canvas (Guestbook)
A non-linear, chaotic space where visitor logs and B&W images appear in random sizes and locations. This "broken grid" allows for overlapping elements, creating a digital collage. Input is handled by a single blinking Neon Acid Green cursor, maintaining the hyper-minimalist aesthetic.

4. Interaction Principles
   Atmospheric Navigation: No standard sticky nav. Movement through the site should feel like moving between rooms in a gallery.
   Data Persistence: Weather and coordinate data are present throughout, anchoring the digital experience to a physical location.
   Brutalist Friction: Minimal UI affordances. Users are encouraged to explore and discover interactive points via the accent-colored cursor.
